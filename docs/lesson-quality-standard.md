# Lingoza Lesson Quality Standard

This is the required design and content standard for every English and French lesson. A lesson is not complete merely because it contains vocabulary cards or a multiple-choice quiz.

## Required learner journey

1. **Understand** — introduce the real-life goal and explain each useful phrase: meaning, when to use it, and any important usage distinction.
2. **See it in context** — give at least one natural everyday example for each core phrase, with a clear translation.
3. **Hear it** — include a listen-and-repeat opportunity and a short, natural dialogue. Listening questions must test details actually spoken in the audio.
4. **Build it** — require the learner to produce a phrase or sentence, not only recognize a correct option. Use personal details or a practical prompt where appropriate.
5. **Use it** — include a realistic response task or role-play in which the learner chooses or creates something suitable for the situation.
6. **Check mastery** — assess both understanding and practical use. Each standard lesson should contain at least 8 meaningful questions or practice prompts, varied by skill rather than repeated paraphrases.
7. **Review and improve** — explain mistakes, show a useful model, and allow the learner to retry before moving on where the skill is essential.

## Requirements for English and French

- Keep the same teaching depth and learner journey in both courses.
- Write idiomatic, level-appropriate English or French; do not translate word-for-word when that sounds unnatural.
- Provide accurate Arabic support when the interface language is Arabic, and preserve the existing English interface when selected.
- Give pronunciation/audio controls for target phrases and dialogues. Browser speech synthesis may be used as a fallback; never imply that it is a recorded human voice.
- Keep examples culturally neutral and useful in everyday life, travel, study, and work.
- Introduce new language in manageable steps and recycle important phrases in later lessons.

## Completion and progression

- A lesson should reward demonstrated learning, not just clicking through slides.
- Essential production/listening tasks must be completed before the learner advances.
- Wrong answers should trigger supportive, specific feedback and a chance to improve.
- Keep existing XP, lesson-unlock, streak, and review systems compatible with lesson improvements.

## Release checklist

Before a lesson is considered ready, verify:
- [ ] The objective is practical and clearly stated.
- [ ] Explanations include meaning and when/how to use the language.
- [ ] Examples are natural and translated accurately.
- [ ] Listening content matches its question and can be replayed.
- [ ] The learner produces at least one original sentence or response.
- [ ] A realistic use-in-context task is included.
- [ ] There are at least 8 varied, meaningful practice prompts.
- [ ] Mistake feedback is specific and retryable.
- [ ] English and French versions have equivalent depth.
- [ ] Mobile layout, navigation, and lesson completion work without regressions.
